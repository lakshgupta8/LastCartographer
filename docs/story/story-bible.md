# The Last Cartographer — Story Bible (v2, Silksong-inspired)

> *A kingdom that forgot how to fly. A map that is forgetting itself. One small bird with a needle-quill, drawing her way back to the centre of the world.*

**What this is.** The master narrative document for an action-adventure metroidvania in the tradition of *Hollow Knight: Silksong*: a fallen kingdom explored freely, gated by abilities, told through sparse cryptic dialogue, environmental storytelling, and bosses that are characters. Every quest, area, enemy, boss, and item should trace to a section here. Section IDs (`SB-4.3`) are referenced from the production plan.

**What we take from Silksong, and what we change.**

| Silksong | Ours | Why it's fresh |
|---|---|---|
| A kingdom of bugs | A kingdom of **birds that have forgotten how to fly** | Movement upgrades are the kingdom's lost memory; the metroidvania structure *is* the theme |
| Silk and song | **Ink and memory** | The world is drawn; forgotten places lose their ink and go white |
| Hornet the hunter with a needle | **Wren the cartographer with a needle-quill** | Same silhouette language, opposite job: she doesn't hunt, she records, and the kingdom punishes her for it |
| Ascending pilgrimage to the Citadel | **Journey inward to the Greyfold**, the white absence at the kingdom's heart | Inversion of the climb; the end is a descent into what was forgotten |
| Painted, hand-drawn, gothic | **Ink-wash on paper**, hand-drawn, Guild-brass and reed-and-stone | Reads as its own thing at a glance; fading is a literal visual state |
| Cryptic NPCs, wishes board, benches | Cryptic NPCs, **Commissions ledger**, **drafting desks** | Familiar rhythm, native vocabulary |

## 0. Pitch

**Logline.** In a kingdom where forgotten places physically vanish, a small cartographer-bird with no memory of her childhood is cast to the far edge of the map and must draw her way back to the centre, where her mentor walked into the white and where the Guild that saved the kingdom is quietly freezing it to death.

**Genre.** 2.5D action metroidvania. Fast aerial combat with a needle-quill, an ink resource that fuels abilities and healing, ability-gated open exploration, boss fights that are characters, quiet story delivered by sparse NPCs and the environment.

**Tone.** Melancholy, hushed, sharp. The kingdom is dying gently; its people are courteous and strange. Violence is fast and clean. Humour is dry and comes from character.

**Themes.**
1. **Preservation versus life.** Keeping something exactly as it was is not keeping it alive.
2. **Who decides what is remembered.** Institutions remember what is useful to them.
3. **Flight is memory.** The kingdom didn't lose its wings; it forgot them.
4. **Home is a practice, not a place.**

**The fantasy.** *I fell off the edge of the map and drew my way back.*

## 1. The Kingdom of Aurenne

### 1.1 Shape
Aurenne is a vertical kingdom built by birds: cliff-terraces, rookeries hanging from overhangs, bridges of rope and bone, towers with no stairs (they were built for flyers), roosts halfway up sheer walls. Since the Grounding, everything has been retrofitted with ladders, hoists, and shame.

The kingdom radiates from the **Halden Plateau** at its centre, where the capital **Halden** sits under the brass dome of the **Observatory**. Around it, the outer regions: the drowned **Saltmarrow** coast to the west, the volcanic **Emberdown** to the north, the great forest of the **Verdance** to the east, the **Windreach** grasslands to the south. And at the very centre, half a day's walk from Halden's walls, the **Greyfold**: a white silence the size of a city, growing by a few wingspans each year.

### 1.2 The Unmaking (41 years ago)
The **Meridian Guild of Cartographers** kept the **Great Atlas** in the Observatory: a vast living drawing, ink on stitched vellum, that the Guild claimed was the kingdom's memory of itself. Guildmaster **Corvin Halloway**, a great owl, attempted the **Complete Survey**: binding every place in Aurenne to the Atlas at once so nothing could ever be lost.

The Atlas shattered into seven **Keystones**. Two things happened that night:
- The kingdom started to **fade**. Any place no living bird remembers loses its ink, dulls, softens, and becomes white paper: the **Blank**.
- Every bird in Aurenne fell out of the sky. The **Grounding**. Nobody has flown since. Nobody remembers how. Fledglings still leap from the roosts; the old bind their wings so they won't try.

### 1.3 Anchoring
Within a year the Guild found that a trained cartographer could **anchor** a place: survey it (walk its bounds, sight its landmarks from vantage points), bind it (take a true memory from someone who lives there, spoken aloud, written in ink), and seal it with the cartographer's mark. Anchored places stop fading. The Guild became the only thing standing between every roost and oblivion. Anchoring is sold. Cities pay; villages wait.

What the Guild does not say: see section 5.

### 1.4 The Blank
White. The eye refuses to focus on it. Walk in without a **tether** (a cord soaked in memory-ink) and you lose direction in thirty steps and yourself in a hundred. Inside, faded places still exist, drifting like islands, and the birds who faded with them are still there: grey, quiet, half-drawn. They call themselves the **Remnant**.

### 1.5 Culture, quickly
- Pre-industrial. Rope, brass, reed, bone, vellum. Oil lamps. No gunpowder.
- **Memory-ink** is distilled from the **pale iris**, which only grows at fade edges. The Guild and the Ferrymen fight over it.
- Birds swear "by the Drawn and the Undrawn." The old greeting is "wings high," said with a wince.
- Speech is formal and a little archaic. Nobody explains things. Everyone assumes you already know, because everyone in Aurenne is a little bit forgetting.

## 2. Wren

### 2.1 Who she is
- A small **wren**: round, brown-grey, quick, with a tilted head and an ink-blue Guild cowl. Silhouette: small body, long thin **needle-quill** held like a rapier. The quill is black iron, tipped to write; the same tool surveys and kills.
- **Age** 24 in bird-years. Journeyman of the Meridian Guild. Pronouns she/her by default; the player may change name and pronouns.
- **The hole.** No memories before age six. Her first memory is a talon around her wrist, white light, and a voice: *"Look at me. Only at me."* That was **Isolde Marr**, who found her at the Greyfold's edge, carried her out, and raised her.
- **The surname.** Isolde gave her "Halloway" because "the Guild owes that name a debt." Wren doesn't know it is the Unmaker's name.
- **What she can do that nobody else can.** Enter the Blank a short way untethered and return. Isolde told her never to show anyone. And, as the game goes on: remember flight, one piece at a time.

### 2.2 Arc
Wren begins with a borrowed profession and a borrowed name, cast to the far edge of the kingdom with nothing but her quill. Each region gives her back a piece of the sky (an ability), a piece of the map (a keystone), and a piece of herself. She ends not by learning who she was but by deciding what the world will be.

### 2.3 Voice
Wren speaks rarely. When the player chooses a line, it is one of three registers, never labelled: **Surveyor** (precise, curious), **Warden** (blunt, protective), **Drift** (dreamy, unafraid of not knowing). They colour NPC replies and decide the last line of the epilogue.

## 3. Factions

Every faction has a **truth** and a **rot**.

### 3.1 The Meridian Guild of Cartographers
- **Seat:** the Observatory, Halden. **Leader:** Guildmaster **Aurelian Voss**, a grey heron in brass.
- **Truth:** Without anchoring, half the kingdom would be gone.
- **Rot:** Anchoring is sold, and it freezes what it saves (5.1). Voss knows.
- **In play:** Guild **Wardens** (herons and cranes with sighting-lances and brass gorgets) patrol anchored towns and hunt unlicensed surveyors. Wren becomes unlicensed at the end of Act 1.
- **Visual:** ink-blue, brass, seven-pointed compass rose, long legs, long weapons.

### 3.2 The Crown of Halden
- **Seat:** the Bastion. **Leader:** Queen-Regent **Maren Ostrell**, a swan, ruling for her nephew, a cygnet who has been nine years old for forty years.
- **Truth:** Order keeps birds fed. **Rot:** Halden's stability is stillness; nothing has been built in a generation.

### 3.3 The Unwriters
- **Seat:** the Quiet House, a monastery in the roots of the Verdance. **Leader:** Brother **Teodor Ashe**, a mourning dove, once a Guild surveyor.
- **Truth:** Anchored places are prisons. Letting go is a kind of love.
- **Rot:** They erase surveys and dissolve atlases, and their mercy is not always asked for.
- **In play:** **Cantors** (doves and crows with solvent-flasks and bells) un-anchor towns. Their attacks *erase*: hits wipe part of Wren's map until re-surveyed.
- **Visual:** undyed wool, ash-marked brows, solvent-stained feathers, bells.

### 3.4 The Holdfast
- **Seat:** Kettil's Rest, Emberdown. **Leader:** **Old Kettil**, a capercaillie, mine foreman turned town-mother.
- **Truth:** Small communities can hold themselves through ritual: nightly roll-calls, songs that name every street, festivals that walk the bounds.
- **Rot:** Only works for towns small enough that everyone knows everyone. Strangers are a problem, because a stranger is a bird nobody remembers.

### 3.5 The Ferrymen
- **Seat:** the Drowned Quay, Saltmarrow. **Leader:** Captain **Sable**, a cormorant.
- **Truth:** The Blank can be crossed. The Remnant exist.
- **Rot:** They sell tethers to the desperate and have stopped counting who comes back.

### 3.6 The Windreach Clans
- **Seat:** none; they walk. **Leader:** Speaker **Idrenne**, a crane.
- **Truth:** Places that are lived in do not fade. The clans have never anchored anything and never lost anything.
- **Rot:** They were refused anchoring by the Guild. The philosophy came after.

### 3.7 The Remnant
- **Seat:** drifting islands in the Blank; gathering place **Thessaly Hollow**, Wren's birthplace.
- **Leader:** **the Archivist**: Corvin Halloway, the great owl, the Unmaker, Wren's grandfather, holding the Remnant together with one keystone for 41 years.
- **Truth:** The faded are not dead. **Rot:** He wants to fix it by doing what broke it.

## 4. The map

Aurenne is one connected space. Each region is a large interlinked area with sub-zones, a hub with a **drafting desk** (rest point) and a **Commissions ledger**, vantage points to survey, a fade in progress, secrets behind ability gates, and a keystone. Regions can be tackled in several orders; the ability gates enforce a loose spine.

### 4.0 Movement abilities and where they live
| Ability | Name in-world | Region | What it opens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | Quill, jump, down-strike pogo, Bind (heal) | Prologue | — |
| Air dash | **Wingbeat** ("she remembered one beat") | Saltmarrow | Gaps, the climb to Emberdown |
| Wall-cling and wall-jump | **Talonhold** | Emberdown | Shafts, the road to the Plateau |
| Grapple to anchor-points | **Inkthread** | Verdance | Canopy, Halden's towers with no stairs |
| Glide on updrafts | **Windmemory** | Windreach | The Steppe crossings, Greyfold approach |
| Enter fades untethered | **Clarity** | Greyfold edge (Act 1 end; grows in Act 3) | White patches hidden across every region |
| Brief true flight | **The Sky** | Endgame only | The final approach; epilogue |

### 4.1 Saltmarrow (start)
- **Look:** tidal marsh, stilt-roosts, boardwalks, reed beds, a horizon that dissolves to white where the sea-fades begin. Half-there lighthouses. Silver, olive, rust; paper texture strongest here (the edge of the map).
- **Mood:** wet, patient, superstitious.
- **Story function:** Wren washes up here after the prologue (7.0). Teaches combat, survey, the Blank as a thing that can be crossed. First Remnant.
- **Hub:** the **Drowned Quay** (Ferrymen port on a half-faded town).
- **Sub-zones:** Reedmother's Roots; the Lantern Chain (seven lighthouses; three faded); the Salt Chapel; the Bone Bridge (a faded whale); the Pale Iris Fields; **Merrow's End** (fading fishing village; its last elder **Dotha** is dying).
- **Ability:** **Wingbeat**, taken from the fourth lighthouse's keeper (boss 6.1) whose lamp "still remembers the flock."
- **Keystone:** in the faded third lighthouse, reachable by tether. Sable's brother **Aury** is there, faded. The keystone is in his wings.
- **Regional decision:** Merrow's End: anchor it (freeze), teach it the Holdfast way (needs Emberdown first), or let it go and lead its people out.

### 4.2 Emberdown
- **Look:** black basalt highland, red-lit mine mouths, hot springs steaming in cold air, ash falling like slow snow, roosts cut into cliff faces. Charcoal, ember orange, sulphur.
- **Mood:** stubborn, loud, communal.
- **Story function:** the alternative to the Guild. Teaches the **bounds-walk**.
- **Hub:** **Kettil's Rest**.
- **Sub-zones:** the Furnace Stair; the Roll-Call Bell; the Nine Chimneys; the Cinder Baths; the Overlook (first sight of the Greyfold from outside); **Hollowvein** (collapsed mine; the families still remember it).
- **Ability:** **Talonhold**, learned from Kettil's daughter **Runa**, who climbs the shafts the old way.
- **Keystone:** at the bottom of Hollowvein. It caused the collapse. Kettil has kept it buried.
- **Regional decision:** Hollowvein: leave it buried, or lead a bounds-walk down into it (boss 6.4) to recover the dead and let the families grieve.

### 4.3 The Verdance
- **Look:** cathedral forest. Trees 80 wingspans tall, light in shafts, moss, mist, ruins of the flying age grown over. The Quiet House is a monastery grown into the roots of one tree. Deep green, gold, bone-white.
- **Mood:** reverent, near-silent, unsettling.
- **Story function:** the Unwriters' argument, made honestly. Teodor's grief. The Stillness seen from inside.
- **Hub:** the **Quiet House**.
- **Sub-zones:** the Root Chapel; the Lantern Grove; the Sunken Library (an anchored library where one monk has read the same page for 38 years); the Old Road; the Overgrown Gate; **Aldermere** (a village the Unwriters un-anchored because its people asked; the player arrives on its last day).
- **Ability:** **Inkthread**, taught by Teodor: the Unwriters' solvent-lines, reversed.
- **Keystone:** Teodor has it. He gives it up only if Wren can honestly say why he did what he did (a dialogue check on choices made in Aldermere, not a stat).
- **Regional decision:** Aldermere: attend the last day, or try to stop it (boss 6.6, the Choir).

### 4.4 Halden Reach (the Plateau)
- **Look:** grey stone, copper roofs gone green, terraces, towers built for flyers with no stairs, the Observatory's brass dome. Always late afternoon. Slate, verdigris, warm brass.
- **Mood:** orderly, handsome, wrong. Nobody has moved house in 40 years.
- **Story function:** the Citadel. Reached mid-game. The Guild, the Crown, the two interludes, the Act 2 climax at the Greyfold's edge.
- **Hub:** the **Journeyman's Hall** (Wren's old room; she is unlicensed now and must sneak or fight in until Interlude A resolves).
- **Sub-zones:** the Seven Bridges; the Paper Mills; **Lowmarket** (the district below the walls, fading, "survey scheduled"); the Old Orchard (only place in Halden with fallen leaves); the Bastion; the Observatory; the Vault.
- **Ability:** none new, but Inkthread + Talonhold open the flyer-towers, which hold the Guild's secrets.
- **Keystone:** inside the frame of the shattered Atlas in the Observatory. Act 3.
- **Regional decision:** the Paper Mill Strike in Lowmarket (mediate, side with workers, or stay out). If it fails, Lowmarket is in the Blank in Act 3.

### 4.5 Windreach
- **Look:** endless wind-bent grass, long shadows, sky that is most of the screen, standing stones, the clans' walking-wagons. Straw gold, sky blue, storm violet. Updrafts drawn as visible ink-swirls.
- **Mood:** free, lonely, hospitable.
- **Story function:** the proof that the true ending is possible. The clans hold their places by walking them.
- **Hub:** the **Long Grass Camp** (it moves between three sites across the game).
- **Sub-zones:** the Nine Stones; the Dry River; the Wind Gate; Idrenne's Fire; the Fallen Star (an iron meteorite used as an anvil).
- **Ability:** **Windmemory**, from the Wind Gate, where the clans hold a fledgling-leap ceremony that nobody has survived in 40 years until Wren.
- **Keystone:** the clans have used it as a cooking-stone for 40 years. Idrenne gives it up laughing. No cost. Deliberately.
- **Regional decision:** whether to survey Windreach at all (surveying it would let the Guild anchor it). A Guild surveyor is following the clan in secret (boss 6.9).

### 4.6 The Greyfold
- **Look:** the largest fade. White paper where outlines of buildings appear only in peripheral vision. Colour exists only around Wren (her lantern-radius). Depth of field extreme; parallax layers dissolve.
- **Mood:** dread, then awe.
- **Story function:** the threshold. The prologue starts at its edge. Act 1 ends at its edge. Act 2 ends crossing it. Act 3 is inside.
- **Hub:** the **Edge Camp** (abandoned Guild outpost).
- **Sub-zones:** Isolde's Last Camp; the Half-Cathedral; the Road That Stops; the Mirror Pool; the Threshold.

### 4.7 The Blank
- **Look:** white, then colour blooming in a radius around Wren. Drifting islands of faded places.
- **Hub:** **Thessaly Hollow**, Wren's birth village. Her mother **Ilse** is here, grey.
- **Islands:** Thessaly Hollow; the Old Capital District (with the mirror-half of the Observatory); Aury's Lighthouse; Aldermere (if released); Lowmarket (if the strike failed); Merrow's End (if released); **every place the player left unanchored**. The Blank is built from the player's choices.
- **Keystone:** the seventh, in Corvin's talons.

## 5. Secrets

Each is foreshadowed at least three times before its reveal. Tag plants in dialogue files as `#plant:5.x`.

### 5.1 The Stillness
Anchoring freezes. Anchored places cannot change: new roosts collapse within a season, crops grow the same, and birds who live there long enough stop changing too. The cygnet king has been nine for forty years. Voss's apprentices from 30 years ago are still apprentices.
- **Plants:** the Old Orchard's fallen leaves; identical market stalls; the Sunken Library's monk; a Journeyman's Hall roommate who "sits his exam next spring", eleven years running.
- **Reveal:** end of Act 1, from Isolde's torn pages found in the Old Orchard.

### 5.2 Why Isolde walked in
She stole the sixth keystone from the Guild vault and went to return it to Corvin, and to find Ilse and tell her Wren was alive. She had no tether. She believed Wren would follow and could cross.
- **Plants:** her note; the Guild's haste to declare her dead; the empty vault slot Pell notices.
- **Reveal:** Act 3, at Isolde's Last Camp.

### 5.3 Where Wren is from
Born in Thessaly Hollow *after* it faded. Children born in the Blank belong to both worlds. Isolde found the village drifting near the edge, found a six-year-old who could *see* her, and carried her out. The crossing took Wren's memories. Ilse let her go.
- **Plants:** Wren's first memory; her untethered crossings; the Remnant in the third lighthouse recognising her.
- **Reveal:** Act 3, Thessaly Hollow, from Ilse.

### 5.4 The Halloway debt
Corvin Halloway is Wren's grandfather. Ilse is his daughter. He walked into the Greyfold the night of the Unmaking looking for his family; Thessaly Hollow faded because his holding pulled its thread under. Isolde gave Wren the name so she could one day claim the truth.
- **Plants:** Voss flinching at the surname; the Halloway crest on the Atlas frame; a gravestone in the Old Orchard.
- **Reveal:** Act 3, from Corvin.

### 5.5 Voss's daughter
**Corra**, a heron chick, faded with the Old Capital District the night of the Unmaking. Voss was Corvin's apprentice. He built the Guild so nothing would ever fade again and never once crossed to look for her. She is in the Blank, drawing him.
- **Plants:** a chick's drawing framed in Voss's office; his refusal to send Wardens into the Greyfold; Pell: "the Guildmaster has never surveyed anything."
- **Reveal:** Act 3. Bringing her memory out is what can turn Voss.

### 5.6 The Grounding (new in v2)
Birds did not lose flight. They forgot it. Flight was held in the Atlas like everything else, and when it shattered, so did the memory of the sky. The abilities Wren recovers are fragments of that memory coming back through her because she is of both worlds. In the true ending, everyone remembers, slowly, over years. In the Fixed World, nobody ever does, because the Atlas holds it for them.
- **Plants:** fledglings still leaping; the towers without stairs; the fourth lighthouse's lamp "remembering the flock"; the Wind Gate ceremony.
- **Reveal:** progressively through each ability; stated by Corvin in Act 3.

## 7. Main plot

Structure: **prologue**, **three acts**, **epilogue**. Act 1 is a linear-ish push from the coast. Act 2 is open across four regions with Halden as the hub. Act 3 is the Blank.

### 7.0 Prologue: The Edge (about 30 minutes)
Wren and Isolde at the Greyfold's edge at dusk. Isolde is teaching: survey a landmark, bind a memory (Isolde's own: "the first time I saw you"), seal. The tutorial is characterisation. Then Isolde says *"Don't anchor what I've drawn. Come find why,"* and walks into the white without a tether. Wren follows. Thirty steps. A hundred. The Blank *moves* her. She wakes on the Saltmarrow shore, on the far edge of the kingdom, with her quill, three torn atlas pages, and no map.

### 7.1 Act 1: The Torn Pages (about 5 hours)
**Goal:** get back to Halden. **Regions:** Saltmarrow, then Emberdown or the Verdance (player's choice; both reach the Plateau).
1. **The Drowned Quay.** Sable. The Ferrymen. Wren learns she is a long way from home and that the Guild has declared Isolde dead and Wren missing.
2. **The Lantern Chain.** Wingbeat from the Lamp-Keeper. First Remnant.
3. **Halvard.** The Guild finds her first. She is unlicensed now.
4. **The climb.** Emberdown (Talonhold, Kettil, the bounds-walk) or the Verdance (Inkthread, Teodor, Aldermere). Whichever she picks, the other opens fully in Act 2.
5. **The Plateau.** Halden. The Old Orchard. Isolde's cache: measurements of the Stillness and five names. Reveal 5.1.
6. **The Edge, again.** Wren returns to where she started and steps in. This time she stays herself. **Clarity.** Pell, sent to watch her, sees. **Act break.**

### 7.2 Act 2: The Five Names (about 15 hours, open)
**Goal:** the five names; keystones or not. **Regions:** all four outer regions plus Halden.

Each outer region ends with a **regional decision** and a **keystone opportunity** (see 4.x). Two **Halden interludes** punctuate the act:
- **Interlude A: The Minder's Report.** Pell's report is written. Wren can read it. Pell decides. If sent, boss 6.8 and the Guild turns hostile in every anchored town. If not, it plays slower.
- **Interlude B: The Audience.** Maren summons Wren. She offers the Crown to whoever will "make Halden permanent." Accept, refuse, or tell her about the cygnet.

**Climax: The Threshold.** With whatever keystones she holds, Wren goes back to the Greyfold's edge. Voss is there with Wardens and tethers, finally going in himself. Boss 6.11. Wren crosses.

### 7.3 Act 3: The Drawn and the Undrawn (about 5 hours)
1. **Isolde's Last Camp.** Her complete atlas. Reveal 5.2.
2. **The Lantern.** Inside. Colour follows Wren. Islands built from her choices. Marrow, a grey chick, starts following her.
3. **Thessaly Hollow.** Ilse. Reveal 5.3. Isolde, alive, grey at the edges, cannot leave under her own power.
4. **The Old Capital District.** Corra (6.13), if Wren looks. Corvin (6.14). Reveals 5.4 and 5.6. The choice is laid out: seven keystones remake the Atlas and hold everything forever, sky included, or the keystones go back to the places they came from.
5. **The Return.** The Threshold from inside. Voss, changed or not.
6. **The Observatory.** The frame of the shattered Atlas. The choice (section 9). In the true ending, boss 6.15.

### 7.4 Epilogue
A walk through Halden, then one outer region, then Marrow. Under thirty minutes.

## 8. Side content (by region)

Silksong-style **Commissions** (a ledger at each hub) plus hidden character stories. Tags: **[F]** foreshadows a secret, **[B]** creates an island in the Blank, **[A]** requires a later ability (back-tracking reward).

### 8.1 Saltmarrow
- **Dotha's Last Season** [B]. Sit with the last elder of Merrow's End. Learn its songs. Decide.

## 9. Endings

Unlabelled in-game. Named here for production.

### 9.1 The Fixed World
All seven keystones. The Atlas remade. Nothing will ever fade. The Remnant return in colour. Nobody ever flies, because the Atlas holds the sky for them. A year later: the same bread, the same sunset in Thessaly Hollow. Marrow is bright, beautiful, and has not said a new word.
- **Needs:** seven keystones; Corvin's cooperation.

### 9.2 The Open World (true)
Wren breaks the frame, gives each keystone back to its place, and walks the bounds of the Blank with everyone she has met singing roll-call. The Blank folds into the world. The faded return grey and colour in over years. Anchoring stops working; so does fading. Birds begin, awkwardly, to remember the sky. Marrow laughs and says a word nobody has said before. Wren flies, once, briefly, at the very end.
- **Needs:** at least four keystones; three of the five names alive and allied; witnessed Idrenne's Fire and one of Aldermere or Hollowvein; persuade Corvin (dialogue built from 5.3, 5.4, 5.6). Boss 6.15.

### 9.3 The Unwritten
Keystones to Teodor; the Unwriters dissolve them. Anchoring ends. Fading continues. The world shrinks to what is actively held. Halden empties over years. Windreach thrives. Marrow fades gently, holding Wren's wing, unafraid.
- **Needs:** Teodor allied; attended Aldermere's last day without stopping it.

### 9.4 The Cartographer's Rest (secret)
Zero anchors, zero keystones. Corvin offers: stay. Wren sits in the mirror-Observatory and draws. Epilogue: Isolde in Halden teaching a new apprentice, Wren's atlas on the desk.

### 9.5 Voss
In every ending, if Wren carried Corra's memory out, Voss walks in to find her. If not, a statue stands at the Observatory that wasn't there before.

## 10. Story-to-mechanic bindings

| Idea | Mechanic |
|---|---|
| The world is drawn | The map is Wren's atlas. Unsurveyed areas are blank paper. **Vantage points** (Cornifer-free): stand on one, hold the quill, and the surrounding area inks itself in. |
| Ink is memory | The **Inkwell** (resource, like silk) fills when Wren strikes enemies. Spend it on **Bind** (heal), **Flourishes** (ink attacks), and **Inkthread**. |
| Anchoring freezes | Anchored towns have a visible **held** state: schedules loop, stock never varies, colour grade locked, and Guild Wardens patrol. |
| Erasure | Cantor attacks wipe portions of the map; re-survey to restore. Unwritten zones lose their parallax layers. |
| Communal holding | The **bounds-walk**: a rhythm traversal with NPC chorus; holds a place without freezing; can be taught. |
| The Blank is your choices | Act 3 islands generated from `WorldState` for every unanchored place. |
| Wren of both worlds | **Clarity** meter for untethered Blank sections; grows with story progress; late gates everywhere. |
| Flight is memory | Each movement ability is a fragment of the sky. Fledglings leap in the background of every region; after each ability, one more of them glides a little further. |
| Memory as currency | Bound memories are inventory. Doors, birds, and keystones ask for specific memories. Offering one weakens that place's anchor. |
| Fade pressure | Fade stages 0–4 advance only on story beats, never on a clock. |

## 11. Writing rules

1. **Rule of three** for every secret's foreshadowing.
2. **Nobody says the theme.**
3. **Courtesy is default.** Aurenne's birds are formal and kind. Cruelty is rare and explained.
4. **Choices cost; they do not punish.**
5. **The Blank remembers.** Every choice about a place echoes in Act 3.
6. **Short lines.** Under 20 words. Speeches are earned. Voss gets one.
7. **Cryptic, not vague.** An NPC may withhold, but everything they say must be true and specific. Players should be able to reconstruct the lore from the lines alone.
8. **Tag `#still`** on intentionally repeated lines in anchored towns.
